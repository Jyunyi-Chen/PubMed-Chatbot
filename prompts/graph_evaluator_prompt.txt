### NODE TYPES

Below are the definitions for each node type:

1. Anatomy

* *Definition:* A semantic group that comprises structural components of organisms, including body parts, organs, tissues, cells, cell components, body systems, body regions, body spaces or junctions, embryonic structures, and body substances.

2. Chemicals & Drugs

* *Definition:* A semantic group that includes chemical substances and pharmacologic agents, such as organic and inorganic chemicals, elements and ions, nucleic acids, amino acids and proteins, antibiotics, hormones, vitamins, biomedical materials, clinical drugs, and other biologically active or hazardous substances.

3. Disorders

* *Definition:* A semantic group representing abnormal conditions and pathologic states affecting an organism. 4. Genes & Molecular Sequences

* *Definition:* A semantic group that encompasses hereditary units and their molecular representations, including genes, genomes, and molecular sequences such as nucleotide, amino acid, and carbohydrate sequences

5. Living Beings

* *Definition:* A semantic group consisting of living organisms and organism-based groupings. It includes humans, animals, plants, viruses, fungi, and other organisms, as well as population groups, age groups, family groups, patient or disabled groups, and professional or occupational groups.

6. Physiology

* *Definition:* A semantic group representing normal biological attributes and functions at multiple levels of organization. It includes molecular, cellular, tissue, organ, and whole-organism functions; organism and clinical attributes; genetic functions; mental processes; and general physiologic functions that characterize normal living systems.

7. Procedures

* *Definition:* A semantic group covering planned actions and processes performed for health care, research, or education. It includes diagnostic procedures, laboratory procedures, therapeutic and preventive procedures, health care activities, research activities, molecular biology research techniques, and educational activities understood as procedural interventions.
